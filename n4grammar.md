# 📙 JLPT N4 Grammar Guide
### Minna no Nihongo Lessons 26–50 — Comprehensive Reference

---

## 💡 Quick Tips: How to Ace N4 Grammar Questions

> **🎯 Tip 1: Identify the sentence pattern first.**
> Before looking at the choices, read the full sentence and identify what grammar point is being tested. Look for keywords like のに, ために, ように — these are strong pattern indicators.

> **🎯 Tip 2: Pay attention to verb forms.**
> N4 grammar heavily depends on which verb form comes before the grammar point (dictionary form, た-form, ない-form, て-form, ます-stem, etc.). Getting this wrong = getting the answer wrong.

> **🎯 Tip 3: Watch for particles.**
> Many wrong answers differ by just one particle (を vs が vs に). Train yourself to notice which particle each grammar pattern requires.

> **🎯 Tip 4: Use elimination.**
> If you're unsure, eliminate options that are grammatically impossible (e.g., wrong verb form) before guessing.

> **🎯 Tip 5: Context matters.**
> Many grammar points have overlapping meanings. The surrounding context (formal/informal, positive/negative, speaker's intention) determines the correct one.

## 📖 Lesson 26 — ～んです / ～ていただけませんか

### Grammar 1: ～んです / ～のです (Explanation / Emphasis)

**Meaning:** Used to explain a situation, give a reason, seek an explanation, or lead into a request. It adds a nuance of "the reason is…" or "the thing is…"

**Formation:**

| Part of Speech | Plain Form → | ～んです |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | 行く | 行く**んです** |
| い-Adj | 高い | 高い**んです** |
| な-Adj | 静かだ → 静か**な** | 静か**なんです** |
| Noun | 学生だ → 学生**な** | 学生**なんです** |

**When to use:**

| Situation | Example | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| Explaining why | あしたから旅行**なんです**。 | "The thing is, I'm going on a trip." |
| Seeking reason | どうして遅れた**んですか**。 | "Why were you late?" (I want to know) |
| Leading to request | ～**んですが**、いい店を知りませんか。 | "The thing is... do you know a good restaurant?" |
| Giving background | 頭が痛い**んです**。 | "It's that my head hurts." (explaining symptoms) |

**More Examples:**
- A: どうしたんですか。 → What happened? / What's wrong?
- B: 財布をなくした**んです**。 → I lost my wallet (that's what happened).
- 日本語を勉強している**んですが**、いい辞書を教えてもらえませんか。 → I'm studying Japanese, and... could you recommend a good dictionary?

> 💡 **Quick Tip:** ～んですが is a powerful softener. Use it when you're about to ask for help, make a request, or introduce a problem. It sounds much more natural than jumping straight to the request.

---

### Grammar 2: ～ていただけませんか (Polite Request)

**Meaning:** "Could you please do ~ for me?" — Very polite request form.

**Formation:** Verb て-form + いただけませんか

**Politeness Scale (from casual to most polite):**

| Level | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Casual | ～て | 教え**て**。 |
| Polite | ～てください | 教え**てください**。 |
| More polite | ～てくれませんか | 教え**てくれませんか**。 |
| Very polite | ～てもらえませんか | 教え**てもらえませんか**。 |
| Most polite | ～ていただけませんか | 教え**ていただけませんか**。 |

**Examples:**
- すみませんが、もう一度説明して**いただけませんか**。 → Excuse me, could you please explain one more time?
- 写真を撮って**いただけませんか**。 → Could you please take a photo (for me)?
- いい先生を紹介して**いただけませんか**。 → Could you please introduce a good teacher?

## 📖 Lesson 27 — Potential Form (～できる / ～える・られる)

### Grammar 1: Potential Verbs (Can do ~)

**Meaning:** Expresses ability — "can do ~", "is able to ~"

**Formation:**

| Group | Rule | Dictionary → Potential |
|---|---|---|
| Group I | last う-sound → え-sound + る | 話**す** → 話**せる**, 書**く** → 書**ける**, 読**む** → 読**める**, 泳**ぐ** → 泳**げる** |
| Group II | る → **られる** | 食べ**る** → 食べ**られる**, 見**る** → 見**られる** |
| Group III | する → **できる** | 来**る** → 来**られる** (こられる) |

**Key particle change:** を → **が** (the object takes が in potential sentences)

**Examples:**
- 日本語**が**少し話**せます**。 → I **can** speak a little Japanese.
- この漢字**が**読**めますか**。 → **Can** you read this kanji?
- あしたは早く来**られません**。 → I **can't** come early tomorrow.
- ここでクレジットカード**が**使**えますか**。 → **Can** I use a credit card here?

> 💡 **Important: 見える and 聞こえる are NOT potential forms!**
>
> | Verb | Meaning | Type |
> |---|---|---|
> | 見**える** | Can be seen / Is visible (naturally) | Separate verb |
> | 見**られる** | Can watch / Is able to watch (ability) | Potential form |
> | 聞こ**える** | Can be heard / Is audible (naturally) | Separate verb |
> | 聞**ける** | Can listen / Is able to listen (ability) | Potential form |
>
> - 山が**見えます**。 → The mountain **is visible**. (it naturally enters your eyes)
> - この映画はネットで**見られます**。 → You **can watch** this movie online. (you have the ability)

---

### Grammar 2: ～ができました (Something was built/completed)

**Meaning:** A new facility, thing, or opportunity has come into existence.

**Examples:**
- 駅の前に大きいスーパー**ができました**。 → A big supermarket **was built** in front of the station.
- やっと友達**ができました**。 → I **finally made** a friend.

## 📖 Lesson 28 — ～ながら / ～し / Habitual Actions

### Grammar 1: V(ます-stem) + ながら (While doing ~)

**Meaning:** Doing two actions AT THE SAME TIME. The second verb is the MAIN action.

**Formation:** Verb ます-stem + ながら + main verb

**Rules:**
1. The SAME person must do both actions.
2. The first verb (before ながら) is the secondary/background action.
3. The second verb is the main/important action.

**Examples:**
- 音楽を聞き**ながら**勉強します。 → I study **while** listening to music. (studying is the main action)
- テレビを見**ながら**ご飯を食べます。 → I eat **while** watching TV.
- コーヒーを飲み**ながら**新聞を読みます。 → I read the newspaper **while** drinking coffee.
- 歩き**ながら**電話をするのは危ないです。 → Talking on the phone **while** walking is dangerous.

> 💡 **Quick Tip:** You can't use ながら when two DIFFERENT people do the actions:
> ❌ 母が料理をしながら、父がテレビを見ます。 → (Wrong! Two different people)

---

### Grammar 2: ～ています (Habitual / Repeated Action)

**Meaning:** When used with time expressions like 毎日, いつも, it describes a habitual action.

**Examples:**
- 毎朝ジョギングを**しています**。 → I jog every morning (it's my habit).
- 毎日日本語を勉強**しています**。 → I study Japanese every day.
- この会社で3年間働い**ています**。 → I've been working at this company for 3 years.

---

### Grammar 3: ～し、～し (Listing Multiple Reasons)

**Meaning:** Gives TWO OR MORE reasons to justify a conclusion — "~ AND also ~, so..."

**Formation:** [Reason 1] し、[Reason 2] し、[Conclusion]

**How it works:** し connects reasons that all support the same conclusion. You can list positive reasons OR negative reasons.

**Examples:**
- 地下鉄は速い**し**、安い**し**、地下鉄で行きましょう。
  → The subway is fast **and** cheap, so let's take the subway.
- この部屋は広い**し**、きれいだ**し**、気に入りました。
  → This room is spacious **and** clean, so I like it.
- あの店は高い**し**、おいしくない**し**、行きたくないです。
  → That restaurant is expensive **and** not good, so I don't want to go.
- 出張も多い**し**、試験もある**し**、大変です。
  → I have many business trips **and** exams too, so it's tough.

> 💡 **Quick Tip:** し can use EITHER polite or plain form. In N4, the plain form version is more common in the test.
> Also, you can use just ONE し (you don't always need two):
> 安い**し**、買いましょう。 → It's cheap (among other reasons), so let's buy it.

## 📖 Lesson 29 — Resultant State / Regret

### Grammar 1: ～ています (State Resulting from Action)

**Meaning:** Describes the CURRENT STATE that resulted from a PAST action. The focus is on the state, not the action.

**How it works:** An intransitive verb in て-form + います shows that an action happened and its result remains.

**Examples:**

| Action (past) | Current State (ています) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 窓が閉まった | 窓が閉まっ**ています** | The window IS closed |
| 電気がついた | 電気がつい**ています** | The light IS on |
| 道が混んだ | 道が混ん**でいます** | The road IS crowded |
| かぎが落ちた | かぎが落ち**ています** | The key IS on the ground |

**How to tell progressive from resultant state:**
- 今、食べ**ています**。 → I'm eating now. (PROGRESSIVE — action happening)
- 結婚し**ています**。 → I am married. (STATE — result of getting married)

> 💡 **Tip:** If the verb is an **intransitive change-of-state verb** (開く, 閉まる, つく, 消える, 壊れる, etc.), ～ています usually means a RESULTANT STATE, not progressive.

---

### Grammar 2: ～てしまいました (Completion / Regret)

**Meaning:** Two uses:
1. **Completion** — An action is completely finished.
2. **Regret** — Something unfortunate happened (often unintentionally).

**Formation:** Verb て-form + しまいました

**Examples (Regret — unintentional / unfortunate):**
- 電車に傘を忘れ**てしまいました**。 → I accidentally left my umbrella on the train.
- 大切な書類をなくし**てしまいました**。 → I lost an important document.
- 財布を落とし**てしまいました**。 → I dropped my wallet.
- ケーキを全部食べ**てしまいました**。 → I ate all the cake (and I shouldn't have).

**Examples (Completion — done!):**
- 宿題を全部やっ**てしまいました**。 → I finished all my homework.
- この本はもう読ん**でしまいました**。 → I've already finished reading this book.

**Casual contractions:**
| Formal | Casual |
|---|---|
| 食べてしまった | 食べ**ちゃった** |
| 飲んでしまった | 飲ん**じゃった** |
| 忘れてしまった | 忘れ**ちゃった** |

> 💡 **Quick Tip:** How to tell completion from regret? CONTEXT.
> - If the result is BAD or UNINTENDED → regret
> - If the action is simply DONE → completion
> - 壊し**てしまいました** (broke it) → clearly regret
> - 食べ**てしまいました** → depends! (ate it all = completion OR ate too much = regret)

## 📖 Lesson 30 — ～てあります / ～ておきます

### Grammar 1: ～てあります (Intentional Resultant State)

**Meaning:** Describes a state that exists BECAUSE someone did something ON PURPOSE.

**Formation:** Transitive verb て-form + あります

**Key difference from ～ています:**

| Pattern | Verb Type | Nuance | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ～て**います** | Intransitive | Simply describing a state | 窓が開い**ています**。(The window is open.) |
| ～て**あります** | Transitive | Someone opened it intentionally | 窓が開け**てあります**。(The window has been opened [on purpose].) |

**More Examples:**
- 交番に町の地図がはっ**てあります**。 → A town map has been posted at the police box (someone put it there).
- テーブルの上にお菓子が置い**てあります**。 → Sweets have been placed on the table (someone arranged them).
- 壁に絵がかけ**てあります**。 → A picture has been hung on the wall.
- エアコンがつけ**てあります**。 → The AC has been turned on (by someone).

> 💡 **Memory trick:**
> - ています = "It's like this" (just a state)
> - てあります = "Someone MADE it like this" (intentional action visible)

---

### Grammar 2: ～ておきます (Advance Preparation / Leaving as is)

**Meaning:** Two uses:
1. **Preparation** — Do something IN ADVANCE for a future purpose.
2. **Leave as is** — Leave something in its current state.

**Formation:** Verb て-form + おきます

**Examples (Preparation):**
- 旅行の前にインターネットでいろいろ調べ**ておきます**。 → I'll look things up online **in advance** before the trip.
- パーティーの前に部屋を掃除し**ておきます**。 → I'll clean the room **in advance** before the party.
- 会議の前に資料を読ん**でおいてください**。 → Please read the materials **beforehand** before the meeting.
- ホテルを予約し**ておきました**。 → I booked a hotel **in advance**.

**Examples (Leave as is):**
- そのままにし**ておいてください**。 → Please **leave it as it is**.
- 窓は開け**ておいてください**。 → Please **leave** the window open.

**Casual contraction:** ておく → **とく**
- 買っ**ておく** → 買っ**とく**
- 調べ**ておいて** → 調べ**といて**

> 💡 **Quick Tip:** Think of ておきます as "do and park it" — you do something and let the result sit there waiting for when it's needed.

## 📖 Lesson 31 — Volitional Form / ～つもり

### Grammar 1: Volitional Form ～(よ)う (Let's / I'll)

**Meaning:** Casual suggestion ("let's do ~") or personal resolution ("I'll do ~").

**Formation:**

| Group | Rule | Dict. → Volitional |
|---|---|---|
| Group I | う-sound → おう | 行**く** → 行**こう**, 話**す** → 話**そう**, 飲**む** → 飲**もう** |
| Group II | る → **よう** | 食べ**る** → 食べ**よう**, 見**る** → 見**よう** |
| Group III | する → **しよう** | 来**る** → 来**よう** (こよう) |

**Examples:**
- いっしょに行**こう**。 → Let's go together.
- ちょっと休**もう**。 → Let's take a break.
- 明日から毎日運動し**よう**。 → I'll exercise every day starting tomorrow.

---

### Grammar 2: Volitional + と思っています (Ongoing Intention)

**Meaning:** "I'm thinking of doing ~" / "I've been planning to ~"

**Examples:**
- 将来自分の会社を作**ろうと思っています**。 → I'm thinking of starting my own company someday.
- 来月日本へ行**こうと思っています**。 → I'm planning to go to Japan next month.
- 夏休みにアルバイトをし**ようと思っています**。 → I'm thinking of getting a part-time job during summer break.

> 💡 **と思っています vs と思います:**
>
> | Pattern | Nuance | Example |
> |---|---|---|
> | ～ようと思って**います** | Ongoing plan (decided a while ago) | ダイエットしようと思っています (I've been planning to diet) |
> | ～ようと思い**ます** | Just decided / Deciding now | ダイエットしようと思います (I think I'll start dieting) |

---

### Grammar 3: V(dict./ない) + つもりです (Plan / Intention)

**Meaning:** "I intend to ~" / "I plan to ~"

**Examples:**
- 来月車を買う**つもりです**。 → I **plan to** buy a car next month.
- 今年の夏は旅行する**つもりです**。 → I **intend to** travel this summer.
- あの映画は見ない**つもりです**。 → I **don't plan to** see that movie.
- 大学院に進む**つもりです**。 → I **plan to** go to graduate school.

> 💡 **つもり vs ～ようと思う:**
> - **つもり** = stronger, more decided, like a firm plan
> - **～ようと思う** = softer, more like "I'm thinking about it"

## 📖 Lesson 32 — Advice, Probability & Possibility

### Grammar 1: V た/ない + ほうがいいです (Should / Had better)

**Meaning:** Giving advice — "You should ~" or "You'd better not ~"

**Formation:**
- Positive advice: Verb **た-form** + ほうがいいです
- Negative advice: Verb **ない-form** + ほうがいいです

**Examples:**
- 毎日運動し**たほうがいいです**。 → You **should** exercise every day.
- 早く寝**たほうがいいですよ**。 → You **should** go to bed early.
- 無理をし**ないほうがいいですよ**。 → You **shouldn't** overdo it.
- そんなにお酒を飲ま**ないほうがいいです**。 → You **shouldn't** drink that much alcohol.

> 💡 **Common mistake:** Positive advice uses た-form (NOT dictionary form):
> ✅ 薬を飲ん**だ**ほうがいい ❌ 薬を飲む**ほう**がいい

---

### Grammar 2: ～でしょう (Probably / Conjecture)

**Meaning:** "Probably" / "I think" — expressing a guess based on some information.

**Formation:** Plain form + でしょう

**Examples:**
- あしたは雪が降る**でしょう**。 → It will **probably** snow tomorrow.
- この問題は難しい**でしょう**。 → This problem is **probably** difficult.
- 田中さんは来ない**でしょう**。 → Tanaka **probably** won't come.
- あの人は日本人**でしょう**。 → That person is **probably** Japanese.

---

### Grammar 3: ～かもしれません (Might / Maybe)

**Meaning:** "Maybe" / "It might be" — expressing uncertainty or possibility.

**Formation:** Plain form + かもしれません

**Examples:**
- 約束の時間に間に合わない**かもしれません**。 → I **might** not make it on time.
- あした雨が降る**かもしれません**。 → It **might** rain tomorrow.
- この薬はちょっと苦い**かもしれません**。 → This medicine **might** be a little bitter.
- あの話は本当**かもしれません**。 → That story **might** be true.

> 💡 **Certainty Scale (Most Tested!):**
>
> | Level | Pattern | Certainty | Example |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Low (~30%) | ～**かもしれません** | maybe | 雨が降る**かもしれません** |
> | Medium (~70%) | ～**でしょう** | probably | 雨が降る**でしょう** |
> | High (~90%) | ～**はずです** (L46) | should be | 雨が降る**はずです** |
> | 100% | ～**に違いありません** | must be | 雨が降る**に違いありません** |

## 📖 Lesson 33 — Commands, Prohibition & Quotation

### Grammar 1: Imperative Form (命令形) — Do ~!

**Meaning:** Direct command. Used in emergencies, sports, or by superiors. Generally considered rude in normal conversation.

**Formation:**

| Group | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Group I | う→え | 急**ぐ** → 急**げ**, 走**る** → 走**れ**, 読**む** → 読**め** |
| Group II | る → **ろ** | 食べ**る** → 食べ**ろ**, 起き**る** → 起き**ろ** |
| Group III | する → **しろ** | 来**る** → **来い** (こい) |

**Examples:**
- 急**げ**！ → Hurry up!
- 逃**げろ**！ → Run!
- がんば**れ**！ → Do your best! (common at sports events)
- 早く**しろ**！ → Do it quickly!

---

### Grammar 2: V(dict.) + な (Don't ~! — Prohibition)

**Meaning:** Strong prohibition. "Don't do ~!" Very direct and informal.

**Formation:** Dictionary form + な

**Examples:**
- 触る**な**。 → Don't touch!
- ここで遊ぶ**な**。 → Don't play here!
- 動く**な**！ → Don't move!
- あきらめる**な**！ → Don't give up!

---

### Grammar 3: ～という意味です (It means ~)

**Meaning:** Explains the meaning of a word, sign, or expression.

**Examples:**
- 立ち入り禁止は「入る**な」という意味です**。 → "No entry" **means** "Don't enter."
- 「さようなら」は "goodbye" **という意味です**。 → "Sayonara" **means** "goodbye."

---

### Grammar 4: ～と言っていました (Reported Speech)

**Meaning:** Reporting what someone else said — "~ said that..."

**Formation:** [Quote in plain form] + と言っていました

**Examples:**
- ミラーさんは来週大阪へ出張する**と言っていました**。 → Miller **said** he'll go to Osaka on business next week.
- 田中さんは忙しい**と言っていました**。 → Tanaka **said** he's busy.
- 先生は明日テストがある**と言っていました**。 → The teacher **said** there'll be a test tomorrow.

> 💡 **Quick Tip:** The quote (before と) is ALWAYS in plain form, even if the rest of the sentence is polite. The tense inside the quote matches what the speaker originally said.

---

## 📖 Lesson 34 — ～とおりに / ～あとで / ～ないで

### Grammar 1: ～とおりに (Exactly as ~ / Following ~)

**Meaning:** To do something in the EXACT SAME WAY as a model, instruction, or example.

**Formation:**
- V た-form + とおりに
- V dictionary form + とおりに
- N の + とおりに

**Examples:**
- 先生が言った**とおりに**書きます。 → I'll write **exactly as** the teacher said.
- わたしがした**とおりに**してください。 → Please do it **the way** I did.
- 説明書の**とおりに**組み立てました。 → I assembled it **according to** the manual.
- レシピの**とおりに**作ったのに、おいしくなかった。 → I made it **exactly as** the recipe said, but it wasn't good.

---

### Grammar 2: V た + あとで (After doing ~)

**Meaning:** After completing one action, then doing another.

**Formation:** Verb た-form + あとで

**Examples:**
- ごはんを食べた**あとで**歯を磨きます。 → **After** eating, I brush my teeth.
- 仕事が終わった**あとで**飲みに行きましょう。 → **After** work is done, let's go for drinks.
- 授業の**あとで**先生に質問しました。 → **After** class, I asked the teacher a question.

> 💡 **前に vs あとで:**
> - 食べる**前に** → **Before** eating (dictionary form + 前に)
> - 食べた**あとで** → **After** eating (た-form + あとで)

---

### Grammar 3: V ない + で (Without doing ~)

**Meaning:** Doing something WITHOUT doing something else first / instead of doing something.

**Formation:** Verb ない-form + で

**Examples:**
- コーヒーは砂糖を入れ**ないで**飲みます。 → I drink coffee **without** sugar.
- 辞書を見**ないで**漢字を書きました。 → I wrote kanji **without** looking at a dictionary.
- 朝ご飯を食べ**ないで**出かけました。 → I went out **without** eating breakfast.
- 傘を持た**ないで**出かけて、濡れてしまいました。 → I went out **without** an umbrella and got wet.

---

## 📖 Lesson 35 — Conditionals: ～ば / ～なら

### Grammar 1: ～ば (If ~)

**Meaning:** "If ~" — expresses a general/natural condition. Often used for hypothetical situations, advice, or natural consequences.

**Formation:**

| Type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Group I verb | う→え + ば | 行**く** → 行**けば**, 読**む** → 読**めば** |
| Group II verb | る → れ + ば | 食べ**る** → 食べ**れば** |
| Group III | する → す**れば** | 来る → 来**れば** (くれば) |
| い-Adj | い → **ければ** | 高**い** → 高**ければ** |
| な-Adj / N | + **であれば** / **なら** | 静か**であれば** / 静か**なら** |
| Negative verb | ない → な**ければ** | 行か**ない** → 行か**なければ** |

**Examples:**
- 春に**なれば**桜が咲きます。 → **If** spring comes, cherry blossoms bloom.
- 天気が**よければ**向こうに島が見えます。 → **If** the weather is good, you can see the island.
- もっと練習す**れば**上手になりますよ。 → **If** you practice more, you'll get better.
- 安**ければ**買いたいです。 → **If** it's cheap, I want to buy it.
- 時間が**なければ**タクシーで行きましょう。 → **If** there's no time, let's go by taxi.

---

### Grammar 2: ～なら (If it's about ~ / If you're talking about ~)

**Meaning:** "If (it's about) ~" — used to pick up on what someone said and give advice or information based on that topic.

**Formation:** Noun / Plain form + なら

**Examples:**
- 北海道旅行**なら**6月がいいです。 → **If** it's a Hokkaido trip, June is best.
- 日本語を勉強する**なら**この本がいいですよ。 → **If** you're going to study Japanese, this book is good.
- 安いの**なら**買います。 → **If** it's cheap, I'll buy it.

> 💡 **Complete Conditional Summary (Most Tested Topic!):**
>
> | Conditional | Form | Best for | Example |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **～ば** | Verb え-form + ば | General/natural conditions | 春になれ**ば**桜が咲く |
> | **～たら** | た-form + ら | Any situation (most versatile) | 雨が降っ**たら**行きません |
> | **～なら** | Plain form + なら | Advice based on topic | 北海道旅行**なら**6月がいい |
> | **～と** | Dictionary form + と | Automatic/habitual results | ボタンを押す**と**ドアが開く |

---

## 📖 Lesson 36 — ～ように (Purpose / Change / Effort)

### Grammar 1: V(dict./ない) + ように (So that ~ / In order to ~)

**Meaning:** Purpose — "so that ~" — Used with NON-VOLITIONAL verbs (potential verbs, intransitive verbs, ない-form).

**Examples:**
- 速く泳げる**ように**毎日練習しています。 → I practice every day **so that** I can swim fast.
- 忘れない**ように**メモしました。 → I took notes **so that** I won't forget.
- よく見える**ように**前の席に座りました。 → I sat in the front seat **so that** I could see well.

---

### Grammar 2: V(dict./potential) + ようになりました (Change over time)

**Meaning:** "Has come to ~" / "Has become able to ~" — describes a gradual change in ability or habit.

**Examples:**
- やっと自転車に乗れる**ようになりました**。 → I've **finally become able** to ride a bicycle.
- 日本語が少し話せる**ようになりました**。 → I've **become able** to speak a little Japanese.
- 毎日運動する**ようになりました**。 → I've **started** exercising every day. (new habit)
- 野菜が食べられる**ようになりました**。 → I've **become able** to eat vegetables. (I couldn't before)

---

### Grammar 3: V(dict./ない) + ようにしています (Making an effort)

**Meaning:** "I make it a habit to ~" / "I try to ~" — conscious effort to maintain a behavior.

**Examples:**
- 毎日日記を書く**ようにしています**。 → I **make it a habit** to write a diary every day.
- 野菜をたくさん食べる**ようにしています**。 → I **try to** eat lots of vegetables.
- 夜遅くまで起きない**ようにしています**。 → I **try not to** stay up late at night.

> 💡 **ために vs ように — The #1 Most Confused Pair:**
>
> | Pattern | Use with | Nuance | Example |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **ために** | Volitional verbs (can control) | Clear, intentional purpose | 自分の店を持つ**ために**貯金する |
> | **ように** | Non-volitional / Potential / ない | Hope/aim for a result you can't directly control | 泳げる**ように**練習する |
>
> **Simple test:** Can the subject directly CHOOSE to do it?
> - YES → ために (save money, buy something, study)
> - NO → ように (become able to, not forget, be visible)

## 📖 Lesson 37 — Passive Form (受身形)

### Grammar: V Passive Form (To be ~ed)

**Meaning:** Something is done TO you, or describes an action from the receiver's perspective.

**Formation:**

| Group | Rule | Dict. → Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Group I | う→あ + れる | 読**む** → 読**まれる**, 書**く** → 書**かれる**, 踏**む** → 踏**まれる** |
| Group II | る → **られる** | 食べ**る** → 食べ**られる**, ほめ**る** → ほめ**られる** |
| Group III | する → **される** | 来**る** → 来**られる** |

**Three types of passive:**

**① Direct Passive (action directly received):**
- 子どものとき母に**しかられました**。 → When I was a child, I **was scolded** by my mother.
- 先生に**ほめられました**。 → I **was praised** by the teacher.

**② Nuisance Passive (inconvenienced by an action):**
- ラッシュの電車で足を**踏まれました**。 → My foot **was stepped on** on the crowded train. (annoying!)
- 雨に**降られました**。 → I **got rained on**. (inconvenient!)
- 隣の人にたばこを**吸われました**。 → The person next to me **smoked**. (and it bothered me!)

**③ Objective Passive (neutral description, often historical/scientific):**
- 法隆寺は607年に**建てられました**。 → Horyuji **was built** in 607.
- この小説は多くの人に**読まれています**。 → This novel **is read** by many people.
- 日本語は世界中で**勉強されています**。 → Japanese **is studied** around the world.

> 💡 **Particle pattern for passive:**
> - Active: 母**が**わたし**を**しかる → Mom scolds me.
> - Passive: わたし**が**母**に**しかられる → I am scolded **by** mom.
>
> The doer (agent) is marked with **に** in passive sentences.

## 📖 Lesson 38 — Verb Nominalization with ～の

### Grammar: V(plain) + の + は/が/を (Turning verbs into nouns)

**Meaning:** Makes a verb phrase function as a NOUN in the sentence — "the act of ~ing."

**Uses:**

**① のは — as subject:**
- 絵をかく**のは**楽しいです。 → **Drawing** pictures is fun.
- 朝早く起きる**のは**大変です。 → **Getting up** early in the morning is tough.

**② のが — as object of feelings:**
- わたしは星を見る**のが**好きです。 → I like **watching** stars.
- 片づける**のが**好きなんです。 → I like **tidying up**.

**③ のを — as object of actions:**
- 財布を持って来る**のを**忘れました。 → I forgot **to bring** my wallet.
- 約束する**のを**忘れないでください。 → Please don't forget **to make** a reservation.

**④ のは〜です — for emphasis/clarification:**
- わたしが日本へ来た**のは**去年の3月です。 → **When** I came to Japan **was** March of last year.
- パーティーが始まる**のは**7時です。 → The party starts **at** 7 (that's when it begins).

> 💡 **の vs こと — When to use which?**
> - Both nominalize verbs, but:
> - **の** is more natural in spoken Japanese and with perception/emotion verbs (好き, 見える, 聞こえる)
> - **こと** is preferred in certain fixed patterns (ことがある, ことができる)
> - In N4, both are usually acceptable.

## 📖 Lesson 39 — Reasons: ～て / ～で / ～ので

### Grammar 1: V て (Cause → Emotional Response)

**Meaning:** Because of ~ (leading to an involuntary emotional reaction).

**Examples:**
- ニュースを聞い**て**びっくりしました。 → I **was surprised** to hear the news.
- 合格し**て**うれしいです。 → I'm happy **that** I passed.
- 友達が来**て**うれしかったです。 → I was happy **that** my friend came.

---

### Grammar 2: N で (External Cause)

**Meaning:** Due to / Because of [noun] — an external event or situation caused something.

**Examples:**
- 地震**で**ビルが倒れました。 → The building collapsed **due to** the earthquake.
- 台風**で**電車が止まりました。 → The trains stopped **because of** the typhoon.
- 事故**で**道が混んでいます。 → The road is crowded **due to** an accident.

---

### Grammar 3: ～ので (Because ~ — polite/objective)

**Meaning:** "Because ~" — softer, more polite, and more objective than から.

**Formation:** Plain form + ので (な-Adj/Noun: な + ので)

**Examples:**
- 体の調子が悪い**ので**病院へ行きます。 → I'm going to the hospital **because** I feel unwell.
- 明日テストがある**ので**今日は早く帰ります。 → **Because** there's a test tomorrow, I'll go home early today.
- 静か**なので**よく勉強できます。 → **Because** it's quiet, I can study well.

> 💡 **ので vs から — Key Difference:**
>
> | Feature | ので | から |
> |---|---|---|
> | Tone | Soft, polite, objective | Direct, assertive, subjective |
> | Preferred in | Formal writing, JLPT, business | Casual speech, personal opinions |
> | After な-Adj/Noun | 静か**なので** | 静か**だから** |
> | Usage tip | "Naturally, because..." | "The reason is..." |
>
> On the JLPT, **ので** is generally the "safer" and more commonly correct answer.

## 📖 Lesson 40 — Embedded Questions / ～てみる

### Grammar 1: Question word + か (Embedded Question)

**Meaning:** Turns a question into a noun clause within a larger sentence — indirect question.

**Examples:**
- JL107便は何時に到着する**か**、調べてください。 → Please check **what time** flight JL107 arrives.
- あの人はだれ**か**、知っていますか。 → Do you know **who** that person is?
- どこで買える**か**、教えてください。 → Please tell me **where** I can buy it.

> 💡 **Key:** In embedded questions, the question clause uses PLAIN form and drops the question particle か from the inner question. The か at the end of the clause marks it as an embedded question.

---

### Grammar 2: ～かどうか (Whether or not)

**Meaning:** "Whether ~ or not" — used when there's no question word (yes/no type question inside another sentence).

**Formation:** Plain form + かどうか

**Examples:**
- 台風が来る**かどうか**まだわかりません。 → I don't know yet **whether** the typhoon will come **or not**.
- おいしい**かどうか**食べてみてください。 → Please try eating it to see **whether** it's good **or not**.
- 田中さんが行く**かどうか**聞いてください。 → Please ask **whether** Tanaka is going **or not**.
- 合格した**かどうか**心配です。 → I'm worried about **whether** I passed **or not**.

---

### Grammar 3: V て + みる (Try doing ~ to see)

**Meaning:** To TRY doing something — to see what it's like, what happens, or how it turns out.

**Formation:** Verb て-form + みる/みます

**Examples:**
- この服を着**てみても**いいですか。 → May I **try on** these clothes?
- 一度食べ**てみてください**。 → Please **try** eating it once.
- 日本語で手紙を書い**てみました**。 → I **tried** writing a letter in Japanese.
- やっ**てみます**。 → I'll **give it a try**.

> 💡 **Quick Tip:** てみる implies curiosity or experimentation. It's NOT about "trying hard" (that's がんばる). It's about "trying something to see what happens."

## 📖 Lesson 41 — Giving/Receiving Actions (Formal)

### Grammar: ていただく / てくださる / てやる

**These are the formal/humble versions of the Lesson 24 (N5) giving/receiving patterns:**

| Pattern | Meaning | Relationship | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ～て**いただく** | Have someone (higher) do ~ for me | Me ← Superior (humble) | 先生に直して**いただきました**。 |
| ～て**くださる** | Someone (higher) kindly does ~ | Me ← Superior (honorific) | 奥さんが教えて**くださいました**。 |
| ～て**やる** | I do ~ for someone (lower) | Me → Child/pet | 息子に飛行機を作って**やりました**。 |

**Detailed Examples:**
- わたしは先生に漢字のまちがいを直し**ていただきました**。
  → I **had** the teacher correct my kanji mistakes. (I humbly received the favor)
- 部長の奥さんがお茶を教え**てくださいました**。
  → The manager's wife **kindly taught** me tea ceremony. (she graciously did it for me)
- わたしは息子に紙飛行機を作っ**てやりました**。
  → I made a paper airplane **for** my son. (I did it for someone younger/lower)

> 💡 **Full Giving/Receiving Hierarchy:**
>
> | Casual (N5) | Formal (N4) | Direction |
> |---|---|---|
> | てもらう | て**いただく** | Someone does for me (I request) |
> | てくれる | て**くださる** | Someone does for me (their kindness) |
> | てあげる | て**さしあげる** | I do for them |
> | — | て**やる** | I do for children/pets/inferiors |

## 📖 Lesson 42 — ～ために / ～のにいい

### Grammar 1: V(dict.) / N の + ために (In order to / For)

**Meaning:** Purpose — "in order to ~" or "for the sake of ~." Used with VOLITIONAL verbs (things you can choose to do).

**Examples:**
- 自分の店を持つ**ために**貯金しています。 → I'm saving money **in order to** have my own shop.
- 家族**のために**働いています。 → I work **for** my family.

- 日本語を勉強する**ために**日本に来ました。 → I came to Japan **to** study Japanese.

**Meaning:** "Good for ~ing" / "Useful for ~" / "Used for ~"

**Examples:**
- この靴は山を歩く**のにいいです**。 → These shoes are good **for** walking in mountains.

## 📖 Lesson 43 — ～そうです (Appearance) / ～て来ます

### Grammar 1: V(ます-stem)/Adj + そうです (Looks like ~ / About to ~)

**Meaning:** "It looks like ~" / "It seems ~" — based on VISUAL appearance or impression.

**Formation:**

| Type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | ます-stem + そう | 降り**そう**, 泣き**そう**, 壊れ**そう** |
| い-Adj | (drop い) + そう | おいし**そう**, 高**そう** |
| な-Adj | + そう | 元気**そう**, 便利**そう** |

**⚠️ Exceptions:**
- いい → **よさ**そう (NOT ×いいそう)
- ない → **なさ**そう (NOT ×ないそう)

**Examples:**
- 今にも雨が降り**そうです**。 → It looks like it's **about to** rain.
- このケーキはおいし**そうですね**。 → This cake **looks** delicious.
- 荷物が重**そうですね**。手伝いましょうか。 → Your luggage **looks** heavy. Shall I help?
- 彼女は元気**そうです**。 → She **looks** healthy/well.
- この仕事は大変**そうですね**。 → This job **looks** tough.
- お金がなさ**そうです**。 → It **looks like** they don't have money.

---

### Grammar 2: V て + 来ます (Go do ~ and come back)

**Meaning:** Go somewhere, do something, and COME BACK.

**Examples:**
- ちょっと切符を買っ**て来ます**。 → I'll go buy a ticket (and come back).
- コンビニでお弁当を買っ**て来ます**。 → I'll go buy a bento (and be right back).
- 飲み物を持っ**て来てください**。 → Please bring something to drink.

## 📖 Lesson 44 — ～すぎます / ～やすい・にくい / Making Changes

### Grammar 1: V(ます-stem)/Adj + すぎます (Too much ~)

**Meaning:** Excessively / Too much — something exceeds the appropriate amount.

**Formation:**
- Verb ます-stem + すぎます (食べ**すぎ**ます)
- い-Adj (drop い) + すぎます (高**すぎ**ます)
- な-Adj + すぎます (静か**すぎ**ます)

**Examples:**
- ゆうべお酒を飲み**すぎました**。 → I drank **too much** last night.
- この問題は難し**すぎます**。 → This problem is **too** difficult.
- 食べ**すぎて**おなかが痛いです。 → I ate **too much** and my stomach hurts.
- この部屋は静か**すぎます**。 → This room is **too** quiet.
- コーヒーを飲み**すぎない**ほうがいいですよ。 → You shouldn't drink **too much** coffee.

---

### Grammar 2: V(ます-stem) + やすい / にくい (Easy / Hard to ~)

**Meaning:** "Easy to ~" / "Hard to ~" — describes the inherent ease or difficulty of doing something.

**Formation:** Verb ます-stem + やすい/にくい (conjugates like い-adjective)

**Examples:**
- このパソコンは使い**やすいです**。 → This computer is **easy to use**.
- この漢字は覚え**にくいです**。 → This kanji is **hard to memorize**.
- この本は読み**やすいです**。 → This book is **easy to read**.
- この薬は飲み**にくいです**。 → This medicine is **hard to take**.
- 歩き**やすい**靴を買いました。 → I bought shoes that are **easy to walk in**.

---

### Grammar 3: Adj くします / Adj にします (Make ~ / Change to ~)

**Meaning:** To make something [adjective] — to change a quality.

**Formation:**
- い-Adj: い → **くします** (短い → 短**くします**)
- な-Adj: + **にします** (きれい → きれい**にします**)

**Examples:**
- ズボンを短**くしてください**。 → Please make the pants **shorter**.
- テレビの音を小さ**くしてください**。 → Please make the TV volume **lower**.
- 部屋をきれい**にしてください**。 → Please make the room **clean**.
- 静か**にしてください**。 → Please be **quiet**.

## 📖 Lesson 45 — ～場合は / ～のに (Despite)

### Grammar 1: V/Adj/N + 場合は (In the case of ~ / If ~)

**Meaning:** "In case ~" / "If ~ happens" — used for more formal or hypothetical situations than たら.

**Formation:** Plain form + 場合は (な-Adj **な**場合 / N **の**場合)

**Examples:**
- カードをなくした**場合は**すぐカード会社に連絡してください。
  → **In case** you lose your card, contact the card company immediately.
- コースをまちがえた**場合は**どうしたらいいですか。
  → **If** I take the wrong course, what should I do?
- 地震の**場合は**エレベーターを使わないでください。
  → **In case of** an earthquake, don't use the elevator.

---

### Grammar 2: ～のに (Despite ~ / Even though ~)

**Meaning:** "Although ~" / "Even though ~" — expresses surprise, frustration, or disappointment that the result was unexpected.

**Formation:** Plain form + のに (な-Adj **な**のに / N **な**のに)

**Examples:**
- 約束をした**のに**彼女は来ませんでした。 → **Even though** we made a promise, she didn't come.
- たくさん勉強した**のに**テストに落ちました。 → **Even though** I studied a lot, I failed the test.
- 薬を飲んだ**のに**まだ熱があります。 → **Even though** I took medicine, I still have a fever.
- 高い**のに**おいしくないです。 → **Even though** it's expensive, it's not good.
- 先週直してもらった**のに**また壊れました。 → **Even though** I had it fixed last week, it broke again.

> 💡 **のに vs けど/が:**
> - **のに** → The speaker is SURPRISED or FRUSTRATED. There's emotional weight.
> - **けど/が** → Simply stating contrast, neutral tone.
>
> 彼は日本人な**のに**日本語が下手です。 (Even though he's Japanese, his Japanese is bad — I'm surprised!)
> 彼は日本人です**が**英語が上手です。 (He's Japanese but he's good at English — just a fact.)

## 📖 Lesson 46 — ～ところ / ～ばかり / ～はず

### Grammar 1: V + ところです (Timing relative to an action)

**Meaning:** Precisely indicates WHERE you are in the timeline of an action.

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| V(dict.) + ところです | **About to** do | 今から食べる**ところです**。(I'm about to eat.) |
| V(ている) + ところです | **In the middle** of doing | 今食べている**ところです**。(I'm in the middle of eating.) |
| V(た) + ところです | **Just** finished doing | 今食べた**ところです**。(I just finished eating.) |

**More Examples:**
- 会議は今から始まる**ところです**。 → The meeting is **about to** start.
- ちょうど出かける**ところです**。 → I'm **just about to** go out.
- 今レポートを書いている**ところです**。 → I'm **in the middle of** writing a report right now.
- たった今帰った**ところです**。 → I **just now** got home.

---

### Grammar 2: V た + ばかりです (Just recently did ~)

**Meaning:** "Just (recently) did ~" — emphasizes that something happened not long ago.

**Examples:**
- 彼は3月に大学を卒業した**ばかりです**。 → He **just** graduated from university in March.
- 日本に来た**ばかり**で、まだ何もわかりません。 → I **just** came to Japan and don't know anything yet.
- さっき食べた**ばかり**なので、おなかいっぱいです。 → I **just** ate, so I'm full.

> 💡 **ところ vs ばかり:**
>
> | Pattern | Time frame | Nuance |
> |---|---|---|
> | V た**ところ** | Just THIS MOMENT | Very immediate — seconds/minutes ago |
> | V た**ばかり** | RECENTLY | Can be minutes, hours, days, or even weeks ago |
>
> 今帰った**ところ**です。 → I **just now** got home. (literally just walked in)
> 先月引っ越した**ばかり**です。 → I **just** moved last month. (still feels recent)

---

### Grammar 3: Plain form + はずです (Should be / Expected to)

**Meaning:** "Should be ~" / "It is expected that ~" — expresses strong expectation based on evidence or logic.

**Examples:**
- ミラーさんは会議室にいる**はずです**。 → Miller **should be** in the meeting room.
- 荷物はもう届いた**はずです**。 → The package **should have** arrived already.
- この薬を飲めば治る**はずです**。 → If you take this medicine, it **should** get better.
- あのレストランは安い**はずです**。 → That restaurant **should be** cheap.

> 💡 **Certainty Scale (Complete):**
>
> | Pattern | Certainty | Meaning |
> |---|---|---|
> | かもしれません | ~30% | Maybe / Might |
> | でしょう | ~70% | Probably |
> | **はずです** | ~90% | Should be (based on evidence) |
> | に違いありません | ~99% | Must be (I'm sure) |

---

## 📖 Lesson 47 — ～そうです (Hearsay) / ～ようです (Inference)

### Grammar 1: Plain form + そうです (I heard that ~)

**Meaning:** Reporting information you heard from someone else or from a source.

**Formation:** Plain form + そうです (NO change to the word before そう)

**Examples:**
- 天気予報**によると**、あしたは寒くなる**そうです**。 → **According to** the weather forecast, it will get cold tomorrow.
- 田中さんは来月結婚する**そうです**。 → **I heard that** Tanaka is getting married next month.
- あの映画はとてもおもしろい**そうです**。 → **I heard that** movie is very interesting.
- 北海道は雪が降った**そうです**。 → **I heard that** it snowed in Hokkaido.

> 💡 **THE Two そうです — Most Tested Distinction:**
>
> | Type | Formation | Meaning | Example |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **Appearance** (L43) | ます-stem + そう | Looks like ~ | 雨が降り**そうです** (It looks like it'll rain — I see dark clouds) |
> | **Hearsay** (L47) | Plain form + そう | I heard ~ | 雨が降る**そうです** (I heard it'll rain — someone told me) |
>
> **The key:** Appearance = ます-STEM (降り). Hearsay = PLAIN FORM (降る).

---

### Grammar 2: ～ようです (It seems like ~ / It appears that ~)

**Meaning:** "It seems/appears that ~" — the speaker's inference based on what they observe, sense, or deduce.

**Formation:** Plain form + ようです (な-Adj **な**ようです / N **の**ようです)

**Examples:**
- 隣の部屋にだれかいる**ようです**。 → It **seems like** someone is in the next room.
- 彼は疲れている**ようです**。 → He **appears to be** tired.
- この料理は辛い**ようです**。 → This dish **seems** spicy.
- 外は寒い**ようです**。 → It **appears** to be cold outside.

> 💡 **そうです (appearance) vs ようです:**
> - **そうです** (L43): Based on IMMEDIATE visual impression (I see it now)
> - **ようです**: Based on INDIRECT evidence or reasoning (I infer from clues)
>
> おいし**そう**です。 → It **looks** delicious. (I see it and it looks good)
> おいしい**よう**です。 → It **seems** delicious. (based on what I heard/read/smelled)

## 📖 Lesson 48 — Causative Form (使役形)

### Grammar: V Causative Form (Make/Let someone do ~)

**Meaning:** To MAKE someone do something (force) or to LET someone do something (permission).

**Formation:**

| Group | Rule | Dict. → Causative |
|---|---|---|
| Group I | う→あ + せる | 読**む** → 読**ませる**, 行**く** → 行**かせる**, 話**す** → 話**させる** |
| Group II | る → **させる** | 食べ**る** → 食べ**させる**, 見**る** → 見**させる** |
| Group III | する → **させる** | 来**る** → 来**させる** (こさせる) |

**Particle Rules (VERY important):**

| Verb Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Intransitive verb | Person **を** V-causative | 息子**を**イギリスへ留学**させます**。 |
| Transitive verb | Person **に** Object **を** V-causative | 娘**に**ピアノ**を**習**わせます**。 |

**Examples (Making someone do):**
- 息子をイギリスへ留学**させます**。 → I'll **make/have** my son study abroad in England.
- 先生は学生に漢字を覚え**させました**。 → The teacher **made** the students memorize kanji.

**Examples (Letting someone do):**
- 娘にピアノを習**わせます**。 → I'll **let/have** my daughter learn piano.
- 子どもを好きなように遊ば**せます**。 → I **let** the child play as they like.

> 💡 **Make vs Let — How to tell?** Context! Japanese doesn't distinguish grammatically. You must read the situation:
> - 子どもを塾に行か**せる** → Make the child go to cram school (the child probably doesn't want to)
> - 子どもを公園で遊ば**せる** → Let the child play in the park (the child wants to)

## 📖 Lesson 49 — Honorific Language (尊敬語)

### Grammar: Respectful/Honorific Forms

**When to use:** When talking ABOUT or TO a superior (boss, customer, teacher, elder).

**Four patterns:**

| Pattern | Formation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ① Passive form as honorific | V-passive | 課長は帰**られました**。 |
| ② お + ます-stem + になる | お + ます-stem + になる | 社長はお帰り**になりました**。 |
| ③ Special honorific verbs | Memorize! | 部長はアメリカへ出張**なさいます**。 |
| ④ お + ます-stem + ください | Polite request | しばらくお待ち**ください**。 |

**Special Honorific Verbs (Must memorize!):**

| Normal Verb | Honorific | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 行く・来る・いる | **いらっしゃる** | go / come / be |
| 食べる・飲む | **召し上がる** (めしあがる) | eat / drink |
| 言う | **おっしゃる** | say |
| する | **なさる** | do |
| 見る | **ご覧になる** (ごらんになる) | see / look |
| 知っている | **ご存じです** (ごぞんじ) | know |
| くれる | **くださる** | give (to me) |

**Examples:**
- 先生はもうお帰り**になりました**。 → The teacher has already gone home.
- 何を召し上がり**ますか**。 → What would you like to eat/drink?
- 社長は何とおっしゃい**ましたか**。 → What did the president say?
- こちらでお待ち**ください**。 → Please wait here.

## 📖 Lesson 50 — Humble Language (謙譲語)

### Grammar: Humble Forms

**When to use:** When talking about YOUR OWN actions to a superior. You lower yourself to show respect.

**Two patterns:**

| Pattern | Formation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ① お + ます-stem + します | Standard humble | スケジュールをお送り**します**。 |
| ② Special humble verbs | Memorize! | あした3時に**伺います**。 |

**Special Humble Verbs (Must memorize!):**

| Normal Verb | Humble | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 行く・来る | **参る** (まいる) | go / come |
| いる | **おる** | be / exist |
| 言う | **申す** (もうす) | say |
| する | **いたす** | do |
| 食べる・飲む | **いただく** | eat / drink |
| 見る | **拝見する** (はいけんする) | see / look |
| 知っている | **存じている** (ぞんじている) | know |
| 会う | **お目にかかる** | meet |
| 聞く・訪問する | **伺う** (うかがう) | ask / visit |
| もらう | **いただく** | receive |
| あげる | **差し上げる** (さしあげる) | give |

**Examples:**
- 今月のスケジュールを**お送りします**。 → I will send you this month's schedule.
- あした3時に**伺います**。 → I will visit (you) at 3 o'clock tomorrow.
- 私はアメリカから**参りました**。 → I came from America.
- 資料を**拝見しました**。 → I have looked at the materials.
- 心から**感謝いたします**。 → I sincerely thank you.

> 💡 **The Golden Rule of Keigo:**
>
> | Subject | Use | Purpose |
> |---|---|---|
> | Other person (superior) | **尊敬語** (Honorific) | RAISE them up |
> | Yourself | **謙譲語** (Humble) | LOWER yourself |
>
> **NEVER mix them up:**
> - ❌ 私がいらっしゃいます (using honorific for yourself)
> - ✅ 私が参ります (using humble for yourself)
> - ❌ 社長が参りました (using humble for your boss)
> - ✅ 社長がいらっしゃいました (using honorific for your boss)

## 🏆 Final Exam Tips

### ✅ Top 5 Most Tested N4 Grammar Points:
1. **ために vs ように** — Volitional verbs use ために; non-volitional/potential use ように
2. **～てしまう** — Read the context: bad result = regret, completed = done
3. **Passive vs Causative** — Know formation AND particle rules (に vs を)
4. **Two types of そうです** — Appearance (ます-stem) vs Hearsay (plain form)
5. **Conditionals** — ば (general), たら (versatile), なら (advice), と (automatic)

### ✅ Top 5 Common Traps:

| # | Trap | ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ために with potential verb | 泳げる**ために** | 泳げる**ように** |
| 2 | Honorific for self | 私が**いらっしゃいます** | 私が**参ります** |
| 3 | Appearance そう with いい | **いいそう**です | **よさそう**です |
| 4 | たほうがいい with dict. form | 飲む**ほうがいい** | 飲ん**だほうがいい** |
| 5 | Causative particle with transitive | 子ども**を**ピアノを弾かせる | 子ども**に**ピアノを弾かせる |

### ✅ Study Strategy:
1. **Read** each grammar point and understand the meaning AND nuance
2. **Memorize** the formation rules (especially verb groups)
3. **Compare** similar patterns side-by-side (ために vs ように, そう appearance vs hearsay)
4. **Practice** with example sentences — write your own!
5. **Test** with past JLPT papers, then review every mistake
6. **Special focus** on keigo verb tables — these are pure memorization
